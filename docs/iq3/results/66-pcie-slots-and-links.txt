# Physical slots, bus topology, PCIe links and software stack — measured 2026-11-08

Read-only session on the production host (`strata`). One 600-token city request was used for the
"under load" arm, plus one host-to-device bandwidth probe per device; everything else is a sysfs /
lspci / NVML read. No serial numbers, no addresses, no keys.

Why this file exists: docs/iq3/README.md's hardware table and link section quote numbers from
here. Two of them are easy to misread, so they are measured twice and stated together:

- the **endpoint** links (die → PLX port) do train to 8 GT/s under load, and NVML's
  `pcie.link.gen.max` is 3 on every GPU — that is the *capability*, not the effective link;
- the **uplinks** (slot → PLX switch) never leave 2.5 GT/s, and the measured host-to-device
  bandwidth per die is Gen1-class (1.58 and 3.14 GB/s). The uplink is the cap.

The brief's premise ("the M10 slots are forced to PCIe Gen1 in the BIOS") is therefore
**confirmed** by measurement, and quantified. The engine's `PCIe probe: 11.3 GB/s` line is the
**3060's** link, not the M10's (independently measured here at 12.05 GB/s on CUDA0).

Notes on the two items that are easy to get wrong:

- `pci=realloc` is on the **host** kernel command line, read from `/proc/cmdline` inside the
  container. It is what makes the BARs of two 4-die PLX switches fit; without it the second
  switch does not enumerate.
- `/opt/cmake-venv/bin/cmake` is 4.4.4. The system cmake is 3.25.1, which does not understand
  the CUDA20 dialect that nvcc 12.9 emits. The build puts `/opt/cmake-venv/bin` first on PATH.

The Proxmox VE version (9) is declared in the brief. It is **not** readable from inside the
container (`pveversion` absent, `/etc/pve` not mounted); what is measured here is the `-pve`
kernel and the ZFS root dataset.

Each M10 is a **PLX PEX 8747 48-lane, 5-port switch** with four GM107 dies behind it:

- slot 4 → root port `00:01.0` → PLX `01:00.0` → downstream ports `02:08/09/10/11.0` → dies at

- slot 5 → root port `00:03.0` → PLX `08:00.0` → downstream ports `09:08/09/10/11.0` → dies at
  `0A:00.0`, `0B:00.0`, `0C:00.0`, `0D:00.0`;
- slot 2 → root port `00:02.0` → the 3060 directly at `07:00.0`.

```
--- idle before  11:26:28Z

--- under load t=4s  11:26:32Z
  00:01.0   2.5GT/s, x8      00:02.0   8GT/s, x16       00:03.0   2.5GT/s, x16
  01:00.0   2.5GT/s, x8      08:00.0   2.5GT/s, x16     03:00.0   8GT/s, x8
  07:00.0   8GT/s, x16       0a:00.0   8GT/s, x8        09:11.0   8GT/s, x8
--- under load t=8s  11:26:36Z   (identical to t=4s)
--- under load t=12s 11:26:40Z  (identical to t=4s)
--- idle after  11:26:44Z        (identical to under load: it does not drop back in 4 s)
```

Three facts, all measured:

1. Every **endpoint** trains to 8 GT/s when there is traffic; NVML `pcie.link.gen.max` = 3 is the
   capability. The 2.5 GT/s at idle is the NVIDIA link-power-management downshift.
2. The M10 dies link at **x8** while their `LnkCap` says x16. The x8 comes from the PLX
   downstream ports, which are wired x8 each, so `(downgraded)` is against the card's x16
   capability, not against the slot.
3. The **uplink from the slot to the PLX never leaves 2.5 GT/s**, even while the four dies behind
   it are at 8 GT/s. Slot 4's switch (`01:00.0`) links **x8**, because slot 4 is an x8 slot.
   Slot 5's switch (`08:00.0`) links **x16**, at 2.5 GT/s. Section 10 shows what that costs.

## 10. Host-to-device bandwidth, per CUDA device (the decisive measurement)

`cudaMemcpyAsync` from pinned host memory, 256 MiB x 4 bursts, best of four, in the engine's own
device order (`CUDA_DEVICE_ORDER=PCI_BUS_ID`, `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=4,0,1,2,3,5,6,7,8`):

```

cuda device 1  (M10, 03:00.0, slot 4)                     best  1.58 GB/s   [1.58, 1.58, 1.58, 1.58]
cuda device 2  (M10, 04:00.0, slot 4)                     best  1.58 GB/s   [1.58, 1.58, 1.58, 1.58]
cuda device 3  (M10, 05:00.0, slot 4)                     best  1.58 GB/s   [1.58, 1.58, 1.58, 1.58]
cuda device 4  (M10, 06:00.0, slot 4)                     best  1.58 GB/s   [1.58, 1.58, 1.58, 1.58]
cuda device 5  (M10, 0A:00.0, slot 5)                     best  3.14 GB/s   [3.13, 3.14, 3.13, 3.13]
cuda device 6  (M10, 0B:00.0, slot 5)                     best  3.13 GB/s   [3.12, 3.13, 3.12, 3.13]
cuda device 7  (M10, 0C:00.0, slot 5)                     best  3.13 GB/s   [3.13, 3.13, 3.12, 3.13]
cuda device 8  (M10, 0D:00.0, slot 5)                     best  3.13 GB/s   [3.13, 3.12, 3.13, 3.12]
```

Read against the Gen1 theoretical figures, with the usual ~80% efficiency of a real copy:

| link | theoretical | 80% | measured | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gen3 x16 (3060) | 15.75 GB/s | 12.6 | **12.05** | Gen3 confirmed |
| Gen1 x8 (slot 4 uplink) | 2.0 GB/s | 1.6 | **1.58** | Gen1 x8 confirmed |
| Gen1 x16 (slot 5 uplink) | 4.0 GB/s | 3.2 | **3.14** | Gen1 x16 confirmed |

Four samples per device, spread under 1% on every one. This is why the BIOS-forced Gen1 premise
holds: the dies negotiate Gen3 with their switch, but the switch's uplink to the host is Gen1, and
the copy runs at Gen1 speed.

The engine's own line, for comparison, comes from CUDA0:

```
strata generate: PCIe probe: 11.3 GB/s host->device (best of 11.3 11.2 11.3 11.3) -> pcie_frac 0.31 (default 0.55)
```

11.3 GB/s against the 12.05 measured above: same link. **It is the 3060's figure and says nothing
about the M10 tier.** The brief quotes it next to the M10 bullet; this file records the correct
attribution.

## 12. AER counters

```
11:24Z   0000:09:11.0   TOTAL_ERR_COR 6      PCIe-bridge
11:33Z   0000:09:11.0   TOTAL_ERR_COR 7      PCIe-bridge
11:34Z   0000:09:11.0   RxErr=7  TOTAL_ERR_COR=7
11:34:31Z .. 11:36:11Z (six readings, 20 s apart)   RxErr=7  TOTAL_ERR_COR=7, unchanged
--- GPU devices (class 0x0300xx/0x0302xx) with any AER class set ---   (none)
```

`09:11.0` is the PLX 8747 downstream port in slot 5 whose child is `0D:00.0` (M10, CUDA8) — i.e.
the **second** M10 card. All nine GPU devices have zero correctable errors, in every reading.

The counter went from **4** at the close of the Iteration 8 window (`results/61`) to **6** at this
session's first reading, then **7** three minutes later, where it stayed. Every event is a
correctable `RxErr` (receiver error) on that bridge; none is fatal, non-fatal or uncorrectable, and
no GPU reports anything. This is an observation, not a diagnosis: the measured load in this session
was light (three short requests plus two bandwidth probes), so the rise cannot be attributed to
load. The two events between the I8 close and the first reading were not observed as they happened.

Note on reading it: the sysfs file `aer_dev_correctable` contains both an `RxErr` row and a
`TOTAL_ERR_COR` row, so summing every row double-counts (it yields 14 here for a true 7). Use the
`TOTAL_ERR_COR` row, or `/root/aer.sh`, which does.
